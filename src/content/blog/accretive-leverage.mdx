Accretive leverage represents a situation where borrowing enhances investment returns. The concept combines "accretive" (gradual increase) with "leverage" (borrowed capital). This strategy proves valuable because investors control debt amounts while market forces dictate interest rates and property attributes.

Mortgage rate volatility significantly impacts investment viability. Over five years, rates fluctuated substantially, affecting property valuations and returns. A six percent cap rate property demonstrates how varying interest rates influence outcomes.

### Three Scenarios

Three scenarios illustrate different leverage environments:

- **Limited negative accretion** occurs at higher rates (4.73%), where borrowing reduces returns
- **Non-accretive leverage** emerges at five-year highs (5.61%), substantially diminishing levered yields
- **Accretive leverage** appears at five-year lows (just above 4%), where borrowed funds enhance current cash flow returns

### The Indifference Point

An indifference point exists at 4.47% interest — below this threshold, increasing debt improves returns; above it, additional borrowing reduces performance. Understanding this threshold enables investors to optimize debt utilization strategically.
