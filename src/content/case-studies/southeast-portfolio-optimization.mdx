## The Problem

A regional investor had assembled a portfolio of single-family rentals across multiple
Southeast markets over several years. Each acquisition had been evaluated individually,
but the portfolio as a whole lacked unified operational systems.

The result: inconsistent vendor relationships, reactive maintenance, manual rent
collection tracking, and limited visibility into portfolio-level performance.
Operating expenses were running well above market benchmarks.

## The Process

We started with a comprehensive operational audit across every property, documenting
workflows, vendor relationships, and cost structures. Key findings:

- **Vendor fragmentation** — The portfolio used different vendors in overlapping service
  areas, missing volume discount opportunities.
- **Maintenance lag** — Average time from tenant request to resolution was measured
  in weeks, not days, driving tenant dissatisfaction and turnover.
- **Data gaps** — No centralized view of property-level performance made it impossible
  to identify underperformers early.

From there, we implemented a phased improvement plan:

1. **Vendor consolidation** — Reduced vendor count and negotiated volume-based pricing.
2. **Maintenance workflow automation** — Built a routing system that assigned work orders
   based on location, urgency, and vendor availability.
3. **Portfolio dashboard** — Deployed a real-time analytics dashboard tracking rent
   collection, maintenance costs, and occupancy at the property and market level.

## The Outcome

Within six months of implementation:

- **Operating expenses decreased** as vendor consolidation and process improvements
  took effect across the portfolio.
- **Maintenance response times improved significantly**, with most requests resolved
  within the target window.
- **NOI improved by 18%** through the combination of cost reduction and reduced vacancy
  from improved tenant satisfaction.
- **Decision-making speed increased** as the portfolio dashboard provided instant
  visibility into performance trends.
