The 2017 tax reform legislation changed the realm of acceptable application of IRC Section 1031, limiting its use to real estate transactions exclusively.

### How 1031 Exchanges Work

When investors sell long-term real estate holdings at a profit, multiple tax obligations emerge: federal capital gains taxes, state-level taxes, and depreciation recapture taxes. A properly structured 1031 exchange permits sellers to reinvest sale proceeds into replacement properties while deferring these tax liabilities.

The statute states: "No gain or loss shall be recognized on the exchange of property held for productive use in a trade or business or for investment..."

### Practical Example

A Southern California family purchased a home in 1989 for $425,000. After converting it to a rental in 2012, the property generated insufficient cash flow. Upon selling in 2017 for $1.6 million, they faced potential $450,000 in taxes.

By executing a 1031 exchange and purchasing ten rental homes in San Antonio, they deferred all tax obligations while transforming their annual $17,000 loss into positive $6,800 cash flow post-financing.
